# Drive (serpentine) belt: inspect, and replace if needed. 2005 Accord EX-L 2.4 L

**Inspection is sourced; replacement is DRAFT.** Chilton's 2005 belt replacement article is
V6-only (pack F07); no 2.4 L routing diagram or tensioner range has been pulled yet.

Interval: **inspect** every 30,000 mi or 2 yr (VERIFIED: OM p.203). Status: no record →
inspect now (`maintenance.md`). No replacement interval is listed: replace on condition.
Time: inspect 10 min; replace ~30–45 min (estimate). Car undrivable only during a
replacement.

## Inspect (engine OFF, key out)
1. Find the belt at the end of the engine with the pulleys (alternator, A/C, power steering).
2. Look at the ribbed side with a flashlight, turning nothing by hand near the fan:
   - cracks across the ribs (several per inch = replace: COMMUNITY rule of thumb),
   - chunks missing, fraying edges, shiny/glazed sides, oil or coolant on it.
3. Auto-tensioner: there are usually wear-indicator marks on the tensioner (COMMUNITY:
   common on Honda K-series); the arrow outside the marked range = belt stretched, replace.
   The 2.4 L range itself: **not found** (Chilton checklist 39).
4. Engine running: squeal at start-up or when turning the wheel = slipping belt or a weak
   tensioner. A chirp that a quick spray of water silences for a moment = belt alignment/condition
   (COMMUNITY test).

## Replace (DRAFT, UNVERIFIED for the 2.4 L)
- Part: Honda **31110-RAA-A02** or Gates **K060475** (past chats, **UNVERIFIED**: confirm by
  VIN, `parts.md`).
- **Photograph the belt routing before removing it** (no 2.4 L diagram in the repo; there
  is usually a routing sticker under the hood, Tier 1).
- Relieve tension by turning the auto-tensioner with a long-handle wrench/breaker bar on its
  bolt (Chilton's V6 article names a belt tension release tool, Snap-on YA9317 or
  equivalent); slip the belt off a smooth pulley; release slowly.
- New belt around all pulleys per the photo, tensioner back, belt last onto a smooth
  pulley. Check every rib sits in its groove.
- Run and listen.

**Backyard tool (COMMUNITY):** a serpentine belt tool set (long thin bar + sockets) is a
common parts-store loaner. Why: space at the tensioner is tight and a normal ratchet won't
swing.

## Engine air filter (UNVERIFIED steps: general Honda airbox layout)
1. Engine off. Find the airbox: follow the big rubber intake tube back from the throttle
   body (on the intake manifold) to the black plastic box it connects to. Which side of the
   bay it sits on is not confirmed from a source (the OM p.210 drawing doesn't label it);
   write it here after the job.
2. Undo the cover fasteners (clips or screws: **look before you pry**; write which here).
3. Lift the cover; lift the filter out. Note which way it sat.
4. Wipe the inside of the box with a damp cloth; nothing falls into the clean side
   (intake tube).
5. New filter in the same way, rubber edge seated all round; cover on; every fastener done
   up. A cover that's not sealed lets dirt bypass the filter.

## Cabin filter (steps from Chilton's **Hybrid** article, pack F05; same-generation car,
UNVERIFIED for the 2.4 L)
1. Open the glove box. Release its damper (the arm on the right side) and let the box hang
   down / unhook the stops so it drops past them.
2. Behind it: the filter housing on the evaporator case. Release the cover tabs, slide the
   filter assembly out.
3. Swap the filter in its frame. **Airflow arrow pointing down** (COMMUNITY: most Honda
   cabin filters; the arrow on the new filter wins).
4. Reverse. Check for air leaking around the housing with the fan on high (Chilton Hybrid
   article says to check this).

## Backyard method (COMMUNITY)
- Hold the old engine filter up to a bright light. If you can see light through most of it
  and it isn't oily, it's not the cause of anything, but there's no record, so swap it
  anyway: the filter is cheap and it starts the paper trail a buyer wants.
- Buy both filters from a Honda counter in person with the VIN: it settles the part numbers
  for free and avoids shipping that costs more than the parts (`plans/2026-11-catch-up-maintenance.md`).

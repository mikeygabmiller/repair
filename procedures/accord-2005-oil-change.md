# Oil and filter change: 2005 Accord EX sedan, 2.4 L K24A4, automatic

Sources: Honda owner's manual, "Changing the Oil and Filter", OM pp.214–215 (read
2026-10-28) · oil type OM pp.212–213 · drain bolt torque also in Chilton (`specs.md`)
Difficulty: easy · Time: ~1 h (estimate from `vehicles/2005-accord-ex-i4-at/plans/2026-11-catch-up-maintenance.md`, not from a
manual). The car stays drivable: if anything goes wrong it's a 10-minute fix, not a
stranded car, as long as the parts are bought before starting.

## Tools and parts
- **Oil:** API Premium grade **5W-20** with the API "For Gasoline Engines" seal; Honda
  Motor Oil preferred; synthetic OK if it meets the same requirements (VERIFIED: OM
  pp.212–213). Buy **5 qt**; the change takes **4.4 US qt (4.2 L) with the filter**
  (VERIFIED: OM p.215 and p.274).
- **Oil filter:** Honda 15400-PLM-A02 or Fram TG7317 (**UNVERIFIED**, past chats: confirm
  by VIN at a Honda parts counter, free; `parts.md`).
- **New drain bolt washer:** 94109-14000 (**UNVERIFIED**, past chats; same check). The
  manual says to fit a new washer every time (OM p.215).
- Oil filter wrench. The manual says "a special wrench (available from your dealer) is
  required" (OM p.214); a cap-type filter wrench that fits the filter works the same way.
- Socket for the drain bolt (size not in the manual: write it here after the job), torque
  wrench that reads 33 lbf·ft (the OM p.215 spec), drain pan, funnel, rags, gloves.
- Ramps, or a jack plus **two jack stands**.

## Safety
- **Never get under the car on just a jack.** Ramps, or jack stands on solid points, on
  level ground, wheels chocked. The manual expects a lift for this job (OM p.214).
  Where the jack stand points are: **UNVERIFIED** here; get them from Chilton's jacking
  section (free with a library card) and add them to `specs.md`.
- The oil is hot after step 1. Let it cool a few minutes before pulling the drain bolt.

## Steps (OM pp.214–215 unless marked)
1. Run the engine until it reaches normal operating temperature, then shut it off.
2. Open the hood and remove the oil fill cap (top of the valve cover). Remove the oil
   drain bolt and washer from the bottom of the engine and drain the oil into the pan.
3. Remove the oil filter and let the remaining oil drain.
4. Install the new filter following the instructions that come with it. Clean any dirt
   off the engine's filter mounting surface first.
5. Put a **new washer** on the drain bolt, reinstall it and tighten to **33 lbf·ft
   (45 N·m, 4.6 kgf·m)** (VERIFIED: OM p.215; Chilton agrees, `specs.md`).
   The manual's **29 lbf·ft is the 6-cylinder** figure. Don't use it.
6. Refill with **4.4 US qt (4.2 L)**, including the filter. Pour slowly; wipe up spills
   right away (OM p.212).
7. Put the fill cap back. Start the engine: the **oil pressure light should go out within
   5 seconds**. If it doesn't, shut the engine off and check the work.
8. Let it run several minutes, then check the drain bolt and filter for leaks.
9. Shut it off, wait several minutes, check the dipstick, top up if needed. Never fill
   above the upper mark (OM p.212).
10. Take the old oil to a recycling center in a sealed container (OM p.215). Most chain
    parts stores take used oil free (COMMUNITY: call first).

## After the job
- `log.md`: date, **odometer**, oil brand, filter brand and number, drain bolt socket size.
- `maintenance.md`: update the oil and filter rows. Next oil change in 5,000 mi or
  6 months (severe schedule).
- Put the receipt in `receipts/`.

## Gotchas
- Past chats gave 29 lbf·ft for the drain bolt; that's the V6 number (OM p.215). 33 is
  right for this engine.
- Buy the filter and washer before draining; the car can't be driven without them.

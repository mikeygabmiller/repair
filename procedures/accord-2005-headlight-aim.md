# Headlight aiming (wall method): 2005 Accord EX-L sedan — DRAFT

**DRAFT:** the owner's manual gives no procedure (OM p.226 only says aim was set at the
factory and adjustment should be done by a dealer or qualified mechanic). Every step below is
general aiming practice from memory, labeled UNVERIFIED, until the Chilton aiming article is
pulled (`prompts/muse-chilton-maintenance.md` item N01).
Difficulty: easy. Time: ~45 min including setup (estimate). Car stays drivable.

## Proper method
A shop with an optical aimer (dealer or alignment shop). Ask for the price first.

## Tools
Long Phillips screwdriver (UNVERIFIED for this car: only a 2001-Accord guide and older-gen
forum posts say Phillips; look down the hole first, a hex head needs a socket), tape measure, painter's tape, a towel, flat level ground facing
a wall or garage door, about 30 ft of room, after dark.

## Steps (UNVERIFIED unless noted)
1. Set the car up as driven: tires at cold pressure **32 front / 30 rear psi** (VERIFIED:
   OM p.237, `quick-reference.md`), trunk emptied of heavy items, half a tank or more, and
   someone of your weight in the driver's seat (or a matching weight). Bounce each corner so
   the springs settle.
2. Nose up to the wall, low beams on. Tape a cross on the wall at the **center of each
   low-beam bulb** (one horizontal line at bulb-center height, one vertical line per side).
   Measure the bulb-center height off the ground on the car and confirm the wall line matches.
3. Back straight up until the lenses are **25 ft** from the wall. Same weight in the seat.
4. Cover one headlight with a towel. Adjust one side at a time.
5. Find the adjusters: the holes on top of each housing. Look for arrows molded next to them.
   No label: turn one 2 full turns and watch the wall. Up/down movement = vertical. Count
   the turns so you can go back.
6. **Vertical:** top edge of the bright part of the low beam about **2 in below** the tape
   line (general guidance is 0–4 in at 25 ft; UNVERIFIED for this car).
7. **Horizontal:** usually leave it. If it's clearly off, the hot spot goes on its vertical
   line or slightly right; never left (toward oncoming traffic).
8. Repeat on the other side. Then drive a dark road: road and right shoulder lit, no flashes
   from oncoming drivers.

## Gotchas
- COMMUNITY: the adjuster is a gear turned by the screwdriver tip; if it clicks or slips,
  the gear teeth are stripped, and that side can't be aimed until the housing is replaced.
- Aim after any bulb or housing change; new aftermarket housings are rarely aimed right
  out of the box.

# 5. Struggles and lessons learned

## The axle job (Oct 19-20)

This was the biggest fight so far. You soaked the ball joint in penetrating oil for hours and hit it with a pickle fork and hammer, but the tapered stud would not let go. Then you went for the inner end at the transmission and could not get the pry bar in far enough to pop it. You put the old axle back in and had to check it was safe to drive (inner end seated, axle nut torqued, test drive). The pickle fork tore the ball joint boot, and the next day the ABS light came on.

- **Lesson 1:** hammering a Honda taper just wedges it tighter. What works is tension plus shock: a screw-type puller cranked tight, then a sharp sideways hit on the knuckle/arm right where the stud passes through. Heat (propane 1-2 min or MAP 30-60 sec) is the backup, with penetrant wiped dry first and the flame kept off the ABS wire, brake hose and boots.

- **Lesson 2:** the inner end barely moves while the outer end is still held. Free the ball joint and hub first, then pop the inner end with a sharp jerk on a bent-tip pry bar against the inner CV housing (not the seal or boot).

- **Lesson 3:** the AutoZone loaner separator did not fit the wide control arm. A two-jaw gear puller or the two-hammer trick were the alternatives.

- **Lesson 4:** hitting near the knuckle can kill the ABS sensor even if you never touch the wire.

- **Lesson 5:** you told me plainly that "try harder" is not an answer. You want the tried-and-true shop method. Noted.

- **Lesson 6:** do not start this job the night before a work day. It is your only car.

**Tools bought for it:** EverTough 32mm socket, EverTough torque wrench, Pittsburgh 18" pry bar, 2 lb dead blow hammer, PB Blaster, magnetic tray. Chose the McGraw 70479 air hammer from Harbor Freight (needs ~4-5 CFM at 90 psi; your McGraw 8 gal compressor will struggle with long use). Still on the list: propane torch head, two-jaw puller, CV axle puller/slide hammer (loaner), new axle nut, Honda ATF DW-1 to top off.

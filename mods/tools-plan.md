# Tool buying plan, staged (researched 2026-11-10)

Buy in the order the jobs come up, not all at once. Each stage lists the job it unlocks,
then budget vs name-brand with what you give up. **Prices are snapshots from listings seen
on 2026-11-10 or the dates shown; recheck before buying.** Under the standing rule, tools are
the owner's call (budget brands are fine); the one exception is anything that holds the car
up, below.

Labels: prices and product claims are **COMMUNITY/retail listing** unless marked; NHTSA
recall data is **OFFICIAL RECORD**.

## Stage 0: free first (borrow before you buy)
Chain parts stores lend specialty tools free with a refundable deposit (AutoZone
"Loan-A-Tool", O'Reilly, Advance). Several articles describe the deposit-refund model and a
return window of up to 90 days ([Motor1](https://www.motor1.com/features/807973/why-autozone-loans-tools-for-free/),
[SlashGear](https://www.slashgear.com/1490380/everything-to-know-about-autozone-loan-a-tool-program)).
**Which tools each store stocks varies: call first.** Good loaner candidates for this car:
torque wrench, brake bleeder/vacuum pump, coolant spill-free funnel, serpentine belt tool,
ball joint/tie rod separators, CV axle puller, micrometer, code reader. AutoZone's own page
didn't load from here (403), so the exact list is unconfirmed.

## Stage 4: bigger jobs only if DIY is chosen
Ball joint press, hub bearing press, valve adjustment tools, coolant pressure tester: **rent
or borrow**, or let a shop do it. The October axle job showed these jobs can strand the only
car (`log.md` 2026-10-19). Cost-check every one against a shop quote first.

## The principle that decides "louder"

With a small head-unit amp, the speaker's **sensitivity** (dB at 1 W / 1 m, on the spec
sheet) decides loudness more than its watt rating. Every +3 dB of sensitivity is about
the same loudness gain as doubling amp power. Ignore "300 W peak" on the box; compare
sensitivity. Big gains past that need an amp (Eonon A304 or a 4-channel amp), which is
a separate job.

Deadening helps clarity and mid-bass: the bare door skin rings and cancels the speaker's
back wave. Butyl mat adds mass so the panel stops vibrating. Partial coverage of the outer
skin behind the speaker does most of the work; sealing the large inner access holes turns
the door into a better enclosure (COMMUNITY: general car-audio practice).

e1400D install facts (VERIFIED: JL Audio e1400D owner's manual, via manualsnet.com, read 2026-11-19):
- Fuse **40 A**, within 18 in of the battery positive post. Fuse type AGU or MaxiFuse.
- Power wire **8 AWG minimum**; ground the same gauge, as short as possible (36 in max).
- Remote: amp turns on with +12 V at REM (18–12 AWG wire).
- Inputs: RCA line level **or** a 4-pin high-level (speaker-level) plug for amplified sources.
  So no line-output converter is needed even if the Eonon has no RCA sub out.
- Minimum load **2 ohms**; below 2 ohms is not recommended and may trip protection.
Still unknown, check before paying or installing:

- Wiring kit: printed power-wire gauge, whether a fuse holder, RCA, remote and speaker wire are included.

- **After reassembly the driver door would not open from inside.** Owner: handle has no

  wheel. Diagnosis (UNVERIFIED, from the symptom): the cable's inner wire is hooked, but the
  cable has slack, most likely the outer sheath end not snapped into its slot on the handle
  housing, or the cable looped / out of a door clip. Fix: panel off, lock the sheath end, check
  the route and the 2 handle screws, test the latch (screwdriver-close it, pull the handle 3×)
  before pressing the panel on. Status: not confirmed fixed. Safety item; see open-issues.md.
- **Testing the cracked Kenwood** before using it on the passenger side, stop at the first fail:
  1. Meter Ω across the terminals: pass ≈ 3–4 Ω (UNVERIFIED, usual reading for a 4 Ω speaker);
     OL = open coil, ≈0 = shorted.
  2. Press the cone evenly: scratchy = voice coil rub, junk it.
  3. AA battery tap: clean pop.
  4. Play test on the amp's RF terminals (RF car-end wires off, ends apart), loose on the seat:
     bass track then high-pitched track; listen only for buzz/rattle and a working tweeter
     (loose speakers always have weak bass). Compare against the driver side with the balance.
  Buzz only → thin E6000 on both sides of the crack (COMMUNITY), cure 24 h, retest. Fails 1–3 → junk.

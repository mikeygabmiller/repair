## The principle that decides "louder"

With a small head-unit amp, the speaker's **sensitivity** (dB at 1 W / 1 m, on the spec
sheet) decides loudness more than its watt rating. Every +3 dB of sensitivity is about
the same loudness gain as doubling amp power. Ignore "300 W peak" on the box; compare
sensitivity. Big gains past that need an amp (Eonon A304 or a 4-channel amp), which is
a separate job.

Deadening helps clarity and mid-bass: the bare door skin rings and cancels the speaker's
back wave. Butyl mat adds mass so the panel stops vibrating. Partial coverage of the outer
skin behind the speaker does most of the work; sealing the large inner access holes turns
the door into a better enclosure (COMMUNITY: general car-audio practice).

## Job notes

- Car stays drivable; doors go back together the same day. Time not verified.
- Disconnect the battery before swapping speakers (repo rule).
- Door panels: plastic clips break with age; buy a small bag of replacement clips before
  starting. Clip part number not looked up (check Honda parts catalog by VIN).
- **Owner, 2026-11-16:** OK with changing wiring for this speaker job (buyers won't remove
  aftermarket speakers). Claude's advice: still use the plug-in harness on the doors (about
  $6–9 and faster than splicing, nothing to troubleshoot later); Posi-Products or soldered
  splices at the tweeters, owner's choice. Keeping the factory speakers is optional.
- Door panels hold no airbag on this car (side airbags are in the seats); UNVERIFIED,
  confirm in the owner's manual SRS section before starting. Don't touch seat wiring
  while the SRS light is on.

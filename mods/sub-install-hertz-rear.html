Don't bridge this sub. It's a 2 Ω single coil, and the Hertz only bridges 4 Ω or more (Hertz tech sheet). Most YouTube videos bridge the rear channels. Skip that part. You use one rear channel: REAR L + and −.

The big picture

Two new wires. Everything gray is already in the car.

Why this works: the Eonon sends only bass out of its Subwoofer Output. The Hertz's rear channels are empty right now (your rear speakers still run off the Eonon). One of them, set to bass-only, powers the sub: about 85 W at 2 Ω (Hertz tech sheet).

Parts and tools

Not needed: Y-adapter (Hertz manual p.44: one RCA into REAR IN L is enough), power wire, fuse, distribution block, line-output converter, bass knob.

1Battery negative off

Loosen the negative (−) battery clamp and tuck the cable away so it can't touch the post.

Why: the speaker wire and RCA go near live amp terminals. A slip with power on can blow the amp fuse or the amp. You have the radio code, so this is no problem.

2Find the Eonon's Subwoofer Output

Pull the radio out the same way you did on 11-21. Behind it hangs a bundle of RCA plugs. Two female (hollow) plugs are unused. One of them is the sub output.

✓White, unused: most likely the Subwoofer Output. Its label is half hidden in the photo and reads "…bw… …p…". Read the full label before you plug in. It must say Subwoofer.
✕Green, unused: the Eonon diagram shows green as #10 Front Camera Input. Not audio. Leave it.
FFront Audio Out L/R, already going to the amp. Don't touch.

If neither label says Subwoofer, stop and send Claude a photo of both labels. The Eonon wiring diagram lists it as #9 Subwoofer Output.

3Plug in and route the new RCA

  1. Plug one end of the new RCA into the Subwoofer Output. Use one plug of the pair. Tape the joint so it can't vibrate loose.
  2. Run the cable right beside the existing front RCA, down to the amp. Same path, same trim. Zip-tie it to the front RCA every foot or so.
  3. Leave the other plug of the pair unused (tape it). Your Scosche 12 ft cable has one black and one clear plug per end: use black at both ends so you know which is which.

Keep wires clear of the front seats' undersides. The SRS light is already on. Don't move or tug the yellow connectors under the seats. Follow the existing RCA path.

Why next to the old RCA and not the power wire: RCA cables carry a tiny signal. Running them alongside the thick power wire can pick up engine whine (COMMUNITY). Your front RCA already took a path that works.

4RCA into the amp: REAR IN, left jack

Plug the RCA into the REAR IN, top (L) jack. Hertz prints "MONO IN" next to it. Leave REAR IN R empty.

Why only the left jack: the Hertz manual labels it "REAR IN (L − MONO IN)", and its "3CH: FRONT + SUB" example feeds the sub signal there (VERIFIED: Hertz HCP manual p.44, p.54).

  • Front PASS MODE → HI: the doors stop trying to play deep bass. They get cleaner and can play louder.
  • Rear LO PASS: the knob runs 50–500 Hz. You want about 80 Hz, so start close to the 50 end and set it by ear in step 9.
  • Rear MONO → ON: copies the left input to both rear channels, so it doesn't matter which rear channel you wire.
  • BOOST OFF and LEVELS at MIN protect the sub until you tune it.

6Speaker wire to the amp: REAR L

  1. Pick which conductor is +: most speaker wire has a stripe, ribs or printing on one side. Call that side + at both ends.
  2. Strip 12 mm (0.62 in) (VERIFIED: Hertz manual p.44) and tin the strands with solder so no stray hair can short across.
  3. Into REAR L + (top) and REAR L − (bottom). Tighten the screws and tug each wire.

Why tin it: one loose strand touching the next terminal can put the amp into protect. Tinned ends also hold better in the Pioneer's push terminals.

7Run the wire to the trunk and connect the box

  1. Route the speaker wire from the amp back to the trunk under the carpet edge or sill trim. Keep it out from under the front seats.
  2. With the rear seatbacks out, it can pass straight through the opening to the trunk.
  3. Press the box's spring terminal, push the tinned wire in, let go, tug-test. + to +, − to −.

8Place the box and strap it down

Lay it in the trunk or on the cargo floor. With the seatbacks out, it plays straight into the cabin. Strap it to solid metal points so it can't slide.

A loose 12" box in a hard stop flies forward. Never leave it unstrapped, and don't rely on stick-on Velcro alone.

9Power up, test and tune

  1. Battery − back on. Key on, radio on, volume low.
  2. Check the amp's PWR light is on and SAFE is off.
  3. Play a song with steady bass. Set the radio to about 3/4 of your normal max.
  4. Turn REAR LEVELS up slowly until the bass blends with the doors. Stop at the first sign of a rough or "farty" sound and back off a notch.
  5. LO PASS: if you hear voices or drums' snap coming from the trunk, turn it down toward 50. If the bass feels thin, turn it up a little.
  6. Polarity test COMMUNITY: sit in the driver's seat and listen. Then swap the two wires at the box only, and listen again. Keep whichever has more bass.

Why the swap: if the sub pushes air while the doors pull, the bass around 80 Hz partly cancels. Flipping the sub's wires fixes that. It's not "wrong" either way, it's whatever sounds fuller in this car.

Why stop at distortion: underpowering won't hurt this sub. A clipping amp sends harsh squared-off waves that overheat the voice coil. That's what kills subs.

10Button up and log it

  1. Turn the key off. The amp's light must go out (no drain).
  2. Radio back in, trim back on, zip-tie loose cable.
  3. Tell Claude: odometer, what you paid for the sub and cables, and how it sounds. It goes in the log and ledger.

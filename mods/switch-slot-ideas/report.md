# Spend the Accord's two blanks on the battery first

> **Update 2026-10-26:** the owner reports **no battery drain now**. The ratings below assumed an unresolved drain, so the battery-protection picks (#1, #2, #4, #44) are worth less than rated, and key-off items like dash-cam parking mode (#25) are worth more. Key-on wiring is still the safe default.

**Vehicle:** owner's 2005 Honda Accord EX sedan, 2.4 L I4, 5-speed automatic (catalog code "4 Door EX KA 5AT"). Research date 2026-10-26.

Your EX has **two** blank switch lids in the driver's lower dash cover, left of the steering column under the left vent. The sunroof switch takes the third position in that bank (Honda parts catalog, Tier 3: [HondaPartsNow 2005 4dr EX switch diagram](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/parts-list/2005-honda-accord--4dr_ex-ka_5at/electrical_exhaust_heater_fuel/switch.html)). The blank is **77625-SDA-A01ZA, about $4.39–$4.87**. The best things to put there are not gadgets. They are controls that help with the car's **unresolved battery drain**. The top pick is a key-on **accessory master switch (9/10)** that cuts every add-on at once, so one flip shows whether an add-on is the drain. Next come a **Victron SmartShunt** or **BatteryProtect** cutoff (8–8.5) and a **backup-camera manual-on switch** (8.5), which helps because cargo blocks the rear view. The only genuine Honda switch that does anything in this slot is the **fog light switch 08V31-SDA-1M035**. It comes in the discontinued Honda fog kit, and that kit **requires cutting and drilling the front bumper** ([Honda install sheet AII 24050](https://www.driveaccord.net/attachments/03-4drfogs-pdf.12405/)), so it scores only 5/10 under your no-drilling rule. **No USB, voltmeter or switch insert is confirmed to fit the 2003–2007 blank.** Nobody has published the opening size, so pop one lid out and measure it in millimeters before buying any insert. None of this is urgent: the right-front axle and bearing (`open-issues.md` #0) and the SRS light (#4) come first, and the car should not be driven until the front end is fixed.

**How to read the costs.** Parts prices come from cited listings where one exists and are marked "(listing)". Anything marked "est." is my estimate (UNVERIFIED). Shop labor uses **$150/hr as a planning rate** for a Puget Sound independent or 12-volt shop. That rate is an estimate built from the national PartsTech survey, where the most common rate is $100–$119/hr and about half of shops charge $100–$139/hr ([Ratchet+Wrench](https://www.ratchetandwrench.com/news/researchreports/article/55398631/industry-insights-us-auto-repair-shops-and-labor-rate-trends-2025)), plus a big-metro markup. Sources disagree: AAA quotes the same survey as "almost half" of shops at $120–$159/hr and lists Washington at $249.87/hr, from a source with no stated method ([AAA](https://www.aaa.com/autorepair/articles/average-mechanic-labor-rate-repair-costs-in-your-state-2026)). The survey article ranks higher. A Honda dealer will likely charge more (AAA: often 25% or more above independents). Where Best Buy publishes a flat rate, I used it: dash cam front+rear **$149.99**, backup camera $149.99, mono amp **$199.99**, radar detector $99.99 ([Best Buy install price list](https://www.bestbuy.com/site/services/car-electronics-professional-installation/pcmcat59600050005.c?id=pcmcat59600050005)). "DIY total" adds about $15–25 for a spare lid, an add-a-circuit and wire.

## Two blanks, one lid part number, and a 15-minute panel pull

**Where and how many.** Honda's catalog for the 2005 4-door EX KA 5AT lists the sunroof switch (35830-SDA-A01) and **two** "Lid, Switch Hole" pieces, 77625-SDA-A01ZA, in the same switch bank (Honda parts catalog, Tier 3: [HondaPartsNow EX](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/parts-list/2005-honda-accord--4dr_ex-ka_5at/electrical_exhaust_heater_fuel/switch.html)). The LX shows three lids and no switches ([HondaPartsNow LX](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/parts-list/2005-honda-accord--4dr_lx-ka_5at/electrical_exhaust_heater_fuel/switch.html)). The EX-V6 shows one lid plus the sunroof switch and the TCS Off switch ([HondaPartsNow EX-V6](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/parts-list/2005-honda-accord--4dr_ex_v6-kl_5at/electrical_exhaust_heater_fuel/switch.html)). So the bank has three positions. Your owner's manual puts the moonroof switch left of the steering column and says the TCS switch is "under the side vent" on V6 models only (VERIFIED: owner's manual pp.68, 187). Heated-seat switches on the EX-L and V6 sit in the console, not in this bank, so adding heat would not use a blank (COMMUNITY: [DriveAccord](https://www.driveaccord.net/threads/ex-l-trim-question.550605/)). **2026-11-07:** this car turned out to be an EX-L (dual-zone climate, `profile.md`). The EX-L catalog shows the same two lids plus the sunroof switch, so the blank count doesn't change; but the car may already have factory heated seats, which would make #27 and #50 unnecessary. Check for switches beside the shifter.

**The lid and its panel.** The lid costs **$4.39** at HondaPartsNow and **$4.87** at HondaPartsConnection (MSRP $6.20). It is catalogued for all 2003–2007 Accord sedans and coupes ([HondaPartsNow](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/genuine/honda~lid~switch~hole~77625-sda-a01za.html); [HondaPartsConnection](https://www.hondapartsconnection.com/oem-parts/honda-2003-2007-honda-accord-lid-switch-hole-nh167l-graphite-black-77625sdaa01za)). It snaps into the driver's lower dash cover, 77300-SDA-A01Z_. The black ZA version costs $73.55. The gray ZB (*NH361L*) version likely matches your "Type B" interior, but no price was shown ([HondaPartsNow garnish diagram](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/parts-list/2005-honda-accord--4dr_ex-ka_5at/interior_bumper/instrument_panel_garnish_driver_side.html)). The 2005 diagram lists the ZA lid only for black interiors, while the 2007 diagram lists ZA for all three colors. Confirm your lid color by VIN. **Buy a spare lid for every project.** Cut the spare, not the original, so the car can go back to stock for resale.

**Getting the panel off.** Honda's own steps (VERIFIED: Honda install sheet AII 24050, steps 51–52): remove the **one self-tapping screw**. Pull out on the panel to release the **four clips on the left side first**. Release the **two right-side clips last** by twisting a flat-tip screwdriver between the clip and the dash frame. Unplug the connector behind it. If your car has a separate under cover, it comes off next (three clips, one pin, one holder). The lids then "pop out very easily" from behind (COMMUNITY: [DriveAccord thread 309105](https://www.driveaccord.net/threads/3-blank-spots-under-left-vent-to-left-of-steering-wheel.309105/)). Honda says to disconnect the negative battery cable before wiring, have the radio code (you do), and reset the driver's window AUTO function afterward. My estimate (UNVERIFIED) is 15–30 minutes just to swap a lid, and the car stays drivable. Spare snap clips are 90666-SDA-A01 (listed at $0.61, sold in packs of 10).

**What genuine Honda switches fit.** Honda catalogues only three switches for this bank on US 2003–2007 sedans: the sunroof switch you already have, the **TCS Off switch 35300-SDA-A01** ($22.70), which does nothing on an I4 because the I4 has no TCS, and the accessory **fog switch 08V31-SDA-1M035**. The fog switch is catalogued for all 2003–2007 4-door trims and is **discontinued** ([HondaPartsNow](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/genuine/honda~switch~foglight~08v31-sda-1m035.html)). Its old dealer price was about $21.80. Today a used one on eBay is about $99.95, and that listing is unclear whether it is the switch, the harness or both ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/p/1033811077)). Forum posters say the coupe uses a different switch, 08V31-SDN-1M035 (COMMUNITY: [AcuraZine](https://acurazine.com/forums/4g-tl-2009-2014-123/diy-how-independent-fog-light-control-933988/)). No source showed a CR-V, Odyssey or other Honda switch fitting this slot.

**Yes, the Honda fog kit includes the switch.** The parts list in Honda's sheet for kit 08V31-SDA-100 includes a **fog light harness, a switch harness and the switch**, plus two relays and a 20 A fuse. The switch plugs into 6-pin, 2-pin and 3-pin connectors already taped to the dash harness, so the dash side needs no splicing. The replacement kit is **08V31-SDA-101**, fits 2003–2005 4-doors including your EX, and is discontinued ([HondaPartsNow](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/genuine/honda~foglight~kit~08v31-sda-101.html)). One new-old-stock kit is listed at **$349 or best offer**, with a few screws missing ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com/itm/188250855156)). **The catch:** the same sheet calls for a **20 mm hole saw (five holes per side)**, a hacksaw cutout in the front bumper and drilled light mounts, and says the job is for skilled technicians only. **The fog bulb is H11, 12 V 55 W.** The Honda sheet and the catalog (33165-SAA-003) agree ([HondaPartsNow fog kit diagram](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/parts-list/2005-honda-accord--4dr_ex-ka_5at/accessories/foglight_kit.html)). That settles the "9005 or H11" conflict in `parts.md`: 9005 is your high-beam headlight bulb. The 2006–2007 kit (08V31-SDA-102) uses a different bumper and harness, but it uses the same switch ([AII 32672](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/resources/installation/hpn/accord/2007-accord-4-door-fog-light-kit.pdf)).

## No insert is proven to fit this blank, so measure before you buy

Honda blanks are not all one size. The common "Honda" 36 x 23 mm push switch sold by iJDMTOY for $10.99 lists 2013–2018 Accords and 2014+ Civics, not the 2003–2007 Accord ([iJDMTOY](https://store.ijdmtoy.com/products/factory-style-4-pole-12v-push-button-switch-w-led-background-indicator-lights-2)). Square flush USB ports from Anina come in 44 x 26, 25 x 22, 34 x 23 and 40 x 22 mm cutouts, and the seller tells buyers to measure first ([Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/Anina-Charger-Adapter-Extension-Odyssey/dp/B0CDWSN719)). The best clue to the old size is Carzdeluxe's $50 blank-mount phone holder. It lists the **2003–2007 Accord, 2006–2011 Civic and 2009–2013 Fit** together, which hints that they share an older, larger blank. That is seller copy only, and the product is sold out ([Carzdeluxe](https://carzdeluxe.com/products/phone-holder-fits-2003-2007-honda-accord)). AliExpress sells Honda USB-C/voltmeter sockets in "Old Type" and "New Type" versions, with no dimensions given ([AliExpress](https://www.aliexpress.us/item/3256806199004221.html)). The dealer catalog's 1.8 x 1.3 x 1.0 in "item dimensions" for the lid look like package size, not the opening.

Branded fog switches are no better. FI Performance and ACM Technik list an identical "2003–2007 Honda Accord Fog Light Switch" at **$59.99**, claimed to be plug-and-play. FI shows it **sold out**, and the ACM page now returns 404. Both look like dropship catalogs ([FI Performance](https://fiperformance.com/products/2003-2007-honda-accord-fog-light-switch)). Generic $15 eBay fog switches exist, but owners report aftermarket switches that "doesn't clip into the slot at all" (COMMUNITY: [DriveAccord](https://www.driveaccord.net/threads/oem-foglight-switch-part-number.562235/)). In short, almost every Honda-blank insert is an unbranded AliExpress or Amazon part, which breaks your no-no-name rule. The name-brand route is a **Blue Sea** USB or 12 V socket in a **cut spare lid**. Blue Sea 1016 dual USB costs $25.98–$29.99 and the 4.8 A Blue Sea 1045 costs $45.95–$74.99. Both are round 1-1/8 in sockets, and nobody knows yet whether that hole fits in the lid ([Hardin Marine](https://www.hardin-marine.com/c-2245-accessory-power-and-usb-power-ports.aspx); [NavStore](https://navstore.com/products/blue-sea-1045-12-24v-dual-usb-charger-1-1-8-socket-mount-1045)). Users of other makes say these inserts often need the tabs trimmed (COMMUNITY: [toyota-4runner.org](https://www.toyota-4runner.org/5th-gen-t4rs/283601-volt-meter-fits-dash-anyone.html)). Do that trimming on the spare lid only.

## Wire everything key-on through fuse #9 until the drain is found

The interior fuse box is on the driver's lower left (VERIFIED: owner's manual p.265). The best tap point for switched power is **fuse #9, Front Accessory Sockets, 15 A** (VERIFIED: owner's manual p.269). The manual says the accessory sockets work with the key in ACCESSORY (I) (VERIFIED: p.78), so #9 should be key-on. **Confirm it with a test light** at the box: live with the key in ACC or ON, dead with the key out. The sockets share a **10 A (120 W) combined limit** (VERIFIED: p.103), so keep the add-on on the tap's own small fuse (5–7.5 A). #32 ACC 7.5 A is a backup for tiny loads like relay coils and the camera trigger. The Bussmann BP/HHH-RP-WM add-a-circuit for mini fuses is rated to 10 A and costs $7.97 ([Walmart](https://www.walmart.com/ip/ATM-Add-A-Circuit/52034992)). Honda's catalog calls these fuses "Fuse, Mini," but whether your box takes standard or low-profile mini taps is unconfirmed, so pull one fuse and look ([HondaPartsNow](https://www.hondapartsnow.com/oem-2005-honda-accord-fuse.html)). **Never tap #22 IG SRS, #10 IG OPDS, #23 IGP (engine computer) or #19 IG Fuel Pump.** A fault there can disable the airbags or stop the engine.

Anything over about 10 A (compressor, inverter, fog lights, horns) goes through a **relay** with its own fused battery feed. The dash switch only drives the relay coil. One 2004 owner reports an unused firewall grommet above the gas pedal for running wires through, which is one forum post to confirm by looking (COMMUNITY: [CarAudio.com](https://www.caraudio.com/threads/help-2004-honda-accord-amp-install.566166/)). Ground to an existing body bolt, **never a seat-belt anchor**, and keep clear of the yellow SRS connectors (owner's manual p.33: "Do not tamper with airbag components or wiring for any reason"). **Solder and heat-shrink** your own splices. Use add-a-circuits and Posi-Taps at the car side so **no factory wire gets cut**. Disconnect the battery negative first.

The drain is why key-off loads rank low. A healthy car draws about **50 mA or less** key-off ([Fluke](https://fluke.com/en-in/learn/blog/digital-multimeters/how-to-find-parasitic-battery-drain-with-a-multimeter)). An always-on digital voltmeter can draw 15–30 mA (COMMUNITY: [Bimmerforums](https://bimmerforums.com/forum/archive/index.php/t-2448585.html)). A dash cam in parking mode was measured at about 250–420 mA (COMMUNITY: [DashCamTalk](https://dashcamtalk.com/forum/threads/how-power-magic-pro-works.35038/post-431468)). On your 38 Ah battery (VERIFIED: owner's manual p.275), my rough math (UNVERIFIED) says the dash cam could leave it unable to crank in two or three days. The SmartShunt draws about 1 mA ([Solacity](https://www.solacity.com/product/victron-smartshunt/)) and the BatteryProtect 0.6–1.5 mA ([Bimble](https://www.bimblesolar.com/BatteryProtect65A)). Both are small enough to keep while they help solve the problem. The **first step costs nothing**: a parasitic draw test with a multimeter at the battery negative after a 10–45 minute sleep wait, then pulling fuses one at a time. Many parts stores also load-test batteries free.

## Ideas to avoid, and why they fail here

**Strobe or flashing lights** are illegal in Washington except for listed signal uses (RCW 46.37.280(3), [WA Legislature](https://lawfilesext.leg.wa.gov/law/RCW/RCW%20%2046%20%20TITLE/RCW%20%2046%20.%2037%20%20CHAPTER/RCW%20%2046%20.%2037%20.280.htm)). **Red, blue or green exterior lamps**, **neon or underglow in motion** and **flashing white** are barred for ordinary cars by [WAC 204-21-230](https://app.leg.wa.gov/WAC/default.aspx?cite=204-21-230). No lamp may show red to the front. A **front LED light bar** runs into the two-auxiliary-lamp limit and mounting-height rules in [RCW 46.37.180](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=46.37.180), and it hurts the car's resale look. **Puddle lights** usually mean drilling the doors. A **no-name aftermarket fog kit** ($40–$138 on eBay, [example](https://www.ebay.com/itm/267250157510)) still needs bumper cutting and breaks the name-brand rule. The **$59.99 FI Performance / ACM Technik fog switch** is sold out, its maker is unknown, and fog switches do nothing without fog lights. A **GPS tracker privacy switch** defeats the tracker. An **aftermarket alarm valet switch** means adding an alarm ($229.99 Best Buy install) and another key-off load. A **trailer-light test switch** is pointless with no hitch. A **heated-mirror retrofit** is unconfirmed for the US EX. An **AUX jack or mic mute** adds little next to the Bluetooth adapter. And **any key-off add-on with no cutoff** should wait until the drain test is done.

## Conclusion

Both blanks pay off most as tools for the problem you already have. A master switch plus a camera switch costs under $100 in parts and takes an evening. It turns "something is draining the battery" into a yes/no test and makes the cargo-blocked mirror less of a problem, all on two $5 spare lids, with the originals kept in a bag for resale. The bigger lesson is that the 7th-gen Accord sits in a gap in the market. Honda never sold a USB or power insert for this blank, and modern Honda-sized inserts are the wrong size. Until you measure the opening, every "fits Honda" insert is a guess. Your measurement may be the first published one for this car, so it belongs in `specs.md`. The Honda fog kit is the one genuine upgrade, and its cost is the bumper, not the switch. If the cracked front bumper cover (`open-issues.md` #7) ever gets replaced, that would be the one time drilling a bumper would not damage original parts. That idea is my own and not from any source.

Amp install: what to do next

2005 Accord · Hertz HCP 4 · Eonon HAC1X7A · RCA route. Updated 2026-11-21 with the Eonon wiring diagram and your 16-pin plug photos. Do the steps in order. Each step ends with a check. Don't move on until it passes.

Why it sounded the same

Two things were missing:

  1. The amp's outputs aren't connected to the speakers. The speakers still run straight off the radio.
  2. The amp may have had no signal at all. The front RCA outputs come off a white plug that was hanging loose behind the radio (step 3).

Garden hose: the radio is the faucet, the speakers are the sprinkler, and the amp is a booster pump. Right now the old hose still runs from the faucet straight to the sprinkler, and the pump's outlet goes nowhere.

Before you start

  • Time: plan a half day (4–5 hours) for a first time. Daylight, a day off, warmer weather if you can. my estimate
  • Drivable? Yes, as long as the radio is plugged back in before you drive. It runs the heat and defrost. If you stop halfway, some speakers may be quiet until you finish.
  • Return window: cutting the Eonon's wires (step 8) may make returning it harder. The window runs until about 2027-01-24.

Have ready (ticks save in this browser):

1Gain knobs to MIN, tape the blue wire

  1. On the amp's input end, turn FRONT LEVELS and REAR LEVELS all the way down (counter-clockwise).
  2. Wrap the bare copper end of the loose blue wire behind the radio in electrical tape.
Why: near-max gain makes the amp distort ("clip") once it's connected, and clipping burns out speakers. You'll set the gain properly in step 11.

3Plug in the white 20-pin plug

From the Eonon diagram, the front RCA outputs (#14, #15), the AMP wire (#7) and the subwoofer and AUX leads all hang off one lead with one plug into the radio.

  1. Follow the Front Audio Out L/R cables back. They should end at the white 20-pin plug ("DQ36-20P").
  2. Push that plug into the rightmost socket on the back of the radio until it clicks.
Check: the plug clicked in, and a gentle tug doesn't pull it out.

4Connect the rear RCA

The rear outputs are on a separate small lead, which is why you didn't see them.

  1. Find that lead behind the radio. Look for the 3 RCA ends next to a small white plug, then read the labels.
  2. Scosche 12 ft RCA: the lead labeled Rear Out L → the white RCA on the Scosche. Rear Out R → the red RCA on the Scosche.
  3. Run it to the amp, along the same path as the front RCA and on the opposite side of the car from the red power wire.
  4. At the amp: Scosche white → REAR IN L, red → REAR IN R.
  5. REAR IN button: set it so the rear channels use the rear input. If the rears are silent in step 10, press it the other way. which way is which: unverified
Go by the printed labels, not the plug colors. In the diagram, Rear R looks white and Rear L looks red, the opposite of the usual colors.

5Turn-on wire: test #7, then connect the blue wire

The amp needs a wire that has 12 V only while the radio is on. The Eonon's #7 AMP wire (on the same lead as the front RCAs, see step 3's picture) should be it. Test it first.

  1. Battery connected, amp fuse out for now.
  2. If #7 has a cap or tape on its end, take it off.
  3. Meter on DC volts (⎓). Red probe on the end of #7, black probe on bare metal or battery −.
  4. Radio on: write down the reading. Key off: write down the reading.

6Find the 8 speaker wires with the meter

The idea: a speaker is a coil of wire, so across its two wires the meter reads a few ohms. Nothing else in the harness reads like that. You test at the black 16-pin plug, unplugged from the radio, so the meter reads through the harness and the car's wiring to the real speakers.

Which speaker is which community: touch an AA battery across the two holes of a pair (a short piece of wire in each hole helps) for a split second. The speaker on that pair pops, so listen for which door. The battery nudges the speaker cone, the same way music does, and it's too weak to hurt it. Tap only.

Check: 4 pairs, each reading about 2–8, each popping a different speaker. Write it down, for example: white = left front.
Stop if: you can't find 4 pairs that read 2–8. Send Claude a photo of the plug and your readings. Don't cut anything.

7Run 4 speaker wires from the amp to the radio

  1. Cut 4 lengths that reach from the amp to behind the radio, plus about 2 ft extra each.
  2. Label both ends of each run with tape: LF, RF, LR, RR.
  3. The side with a stripe, ribs or printing is − on all 4 runs.
  4. Route them with the RCA cables, away from the red power wire. Running them next to it can add hum or whine.

8Cut and connect only the left front

Battery − off.

Only the 2 wires you proved are the left front in step 6. If something goes wrong, you'll know exactly where.

  1. Cut each of the 2 wires about 4 inches from the black 16-pin plug.
  2. Short stub on the 16-pin plug = radio end: heat-shrink or tape it off. Long piece = car end (goes to the speaker). Rule that never changes: the piece connected to the radio gets taped, and the piece going to the car gets the amp wire.
  3. Slide heat shrink onto the long piece before soldering.
  4. Solder the LF run to the long pieces: solid (+) → plain side, striped (−) → striped side. Then shrink the heat shrink.
  5. At the amp: loosen the set screws, insert the wire, tighten. Plain side → LF+, striped → LF− (map below).
Never let a speaker − touch the car's metal, the amp's GND, or another speaker's wire. These aren't ground wires. Shorting them can trip the amp's protect mode or damage it.

9Test the left front

  1. Battery − back on. Put the amp fuse back in at the battery.
  2. Radio on at low volume. The amp's PWR light should come on. Then do the turn-off check from step 5.
  3. Radio balance all the way left, fade all the way front.
  4. Turn FRONT LEVELS from MIN up to about a quarter.

Test with balance and fade: each speaker should play alone when you push the sound toward it. Rears silent? Flip the REAR IN button.

11Set the gain with the meter

The idea: the gain knob isn't a volume knob. It matches the amp to the radio's signal. With a steady tone at your loudest listening volume, you raise the gain until the amp puts out what the speakers can take. For the old factory speakers that's 7–8 V, about 12–16 W into 4 Ω (watts = volts × volts ÷ ohms). target from the install plan

  1. Take all 8 speaker wires out of the amp terminals so the tone can't hurt the speakers. Keep the ends apart.
  2. Radio: EQ flat, bass boost off, loudness off, balance and fade centered.
  3. Play the 1 kHz tone. Turn the radio up to about 3/4, the loudest you'd really listen, and leave it there.
  4. Meter on AC volts (~). Red probe on the LF+ set screw, black on LF−.
  5. Slowly turn FRONT LEVELS up until the meter reads 7–8. Stop.
  6. Probes on LR+ and LR−. Turn REAR LEVELS up to 7–8.
  7. Volume down. Put all 8 wires back and tighten them.
Backyard way community: gains at MIN, radio at 3/4 with a song you know well. Turn the gain up until it starts to sound harsh, then back off a quarter turn. You're finding the distortion point by ear. It's less precise than the meter, so err low.

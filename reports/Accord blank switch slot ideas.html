Accord blank switch ideas

2005 Honda Accord EX sedan · 2.4 L 4-cyl · automatic · researched 2026-10-26

Short answer: your EX has two blank switch lids left of the steering wheel. Use them for the battery first. The best pick is a key-on master switch that turns off every add-on at once. Flip it off overnight: if the battery stops draining, an add-on is to blame. Second: a backup-camera switch, so you can see past cargo while driving.

The only genuine Honda switch that does anything here is the fog light switch. It comes in Honda's fog kit, and that kit needs holes cut and drilled in the front bumper.

No USB, voltmeter or switch insert is confirmed to fit this year's blank. Measure first.

Update 2026-10-26: the owner reports no battery drain now. Ratings assumed an unresolved drain, so the battery picks (#1, #2, #4, #44) are worth less than shown and key-off items like dash-cam parking mode (#25) are worth more. Key-on wiring is still the safe default.

Not urgent. Fix the right-front axle and bearing (open issue #0) and look into the SRS light (#4) first. Don't drive the car until the front end is fixed.

Start here

  1. Free parasitic-draw test with a multimeter at the battery negative. Wait 10–45 min for the car to sleep, then pull fuses one at a time. Parts stores load-test the battery free.
  2. Accessory master switch + "add-ons on" light in blank #1. 9/10. About $40–70 DIY.
  3. Backup-camera mode switch (always-on or reverse-only) in blank #2. 8–8.5/10. About $20–35 DIY.
  4. Victron SmartShunt (logs the drain) or BatteryProtect (auto cutoff) under the hood. 8–8.5/10.
  5. Cargo-area LED wired through the master switch. 8/10. Or keep a blank stock (8/10).

Key facts

  • Where: the switch bank in the driver's lower dash cover, left of the steering column, under the left vent. OEM CATALOG
  • How many: 3 positions. On the EX the sunroof switch takes one, so 2 are blank. The LX has 3 blanks and the EX-V6 has 1. Heated-seat switches are in the console, not here. OEM CATALOG
  • Blank lid part number: 77625-SDA-A01ZA (Graphite Black), about $4.39–$4.87 (MSRP $6.20). Confirm the color for your gray/black interior by VIN. OEM CATALOG
  • Genuine Honda switches for this bank: sunroof (you have it), TCS Off 35300-SDA-A01 (V6 only, does nothing on an I4), and fog switch 08V31-SDA-1M035 (discontinued, about $100 used). OEM CATALOG
  • Fog kit: Honda kit 08V31-SDA-101 (2003–05 sedan, discontinued, about $349 new-old-stock) includes the switch and harness, which plug into connectors already in the dash. It requires a 20 mm hole saw and cuts in the bumper, and Honda says skilled technicians only. Bulbs are H11 12 V 55 W. VERIFIED: Honda AII 24050
  • Opening size: unknown. No source gives it. Pop a lid out and measure it in mm before buying any insert. Modern "Honda" 36 x 23 mm switches are for 2013+ Accords.
  • No insert product is confirmed to fit the 2003–2007 blank. Most are no-name. Do any cutting on a spare $5 lid and keep the original.

Removing the panel (Honda's steps)

  1. Disconnect the battery negative if you will wire anything. You have the radio code.
  2. Remove the one self-tapping screw in the driver's lower dash cover.
  3. Pull outward to release the 4 clips on the left side first.
  4. Release the 2 right-side clips last by twisting a flat screwdriver between the clip and the dash frame. A plastic trim tool is gentler.
  5. Unplug the connector behind it and lift the cover off. The lids pop out from behind.
  6. Afterward, reset the driver's window AUTO: hold it down until fully open, then pull it up until closed and hold 2 seconds.

Just swapping a lid takes about 15–30 min (estimate), and the car stays drivable. Spare clips: 90666-SDA-A01.

Wiring basics

  • Tap fuse #9, Front Accessory Sockets, 15 A with an add-a-circuit (Bussmann mini, about $8). The manual says the sockets work with the key in ACCESSORY, so it should be key-on (owner's manual p.78, fuse chart p.269). VERIFIED
  • Confirm with a test light: dead with the key out, live in ACC and ON. Check whether the box takes standard or low-profile mini fuses.
  • Put your add-on on the tap's own 5–7.5 A fuse. The sockets share a 10 A limit (p.103).
  • Never tap #22 IG SRS, #10 IG OPDS, #23 IGP (engine computer) or #19 fuel pump.
  • Loads over about 10 A (compressor, inverter, fogs, horns) need a relay and their own fused battery feed. The dash switch only runs the relay coil.
  • Solder and heat-shrink your splices. Use add-a-circuits and Posi-Taps so no factory wire is cut.
  • Disconnect the battery before wiring. Ground to an existing body bolt, never a seat-belt bolt. Stay away from yellow SRS connectors.
  • Do any cutting or drilling on a spare blank so the original stays stock.
  • Until the drain is found, wire everything key-on. A parking-mode dash cam (~0.25–0.42 A) can flatten this 38 Ah battery in days.

Ideas to avoid

  • Strobe or flashing lights: illegal in WA (RCW 46.37.280).
  • Red, blue or green exterior lights, neon or underglow while driving, flashing white: barred by WAC 204-21-230. No red visible from the front.
  • Front LED light bar: WA allows only 2 aux lamps, at set heights (RCW 46.37.180). Hurts resale.
  • Puddle lights: usually mean drilling the doors.
  • No-name aftermarket fog kits ($40–$138): still cut the bumper, and they aren't name brand.
  • $59.99 FI Performance / ACM Technik fog switch: sold out, maker unknown, useless without fog lights.
  • DIY seat heater: side airbag in the seat, and your SRS light is already on. Never DIY.
  • GPS "privacy" switch, alarm valet switch, trailer-light tester, heated-mirror retrofit, AUX jack, mic mute: pointless or unconfirmed for this car.
  • Any add-on that stays on key-off with no cutoff, until the drain test is done.
